How Drop Coverage compares with other NBA prediction and prop sites

Drop Coverage publishes a prediction for every NBA player in every game, sets it against Australian bookmaker lines, and keeps every graded result on a public record measured against the closing line. Most NBA betting sites do one of those three things. This page sets out what each kind of site is for, what we do differently, where we are weaker, and eight questions worth asking of any service before you trust its numbers.

Written 25 November 2026. Our own figures below are from that day; the live ones are on the track record.

What Drop Coverage does differently

  • A prediction for every player, not a shortlist. Points, rebounds, assists, threes and the combined markets for every player expected to play, each with the range the result lands inside 80% of the time. The board covers the whole slate.
  • Australian lines, Australian time. Lines and prices are from Australian bookmakers (Sportsbet, PointsBet, Neds, Ladbrokes and Unibet) in decimal odds, and every tip-off is shown in Australian eastern time. US visitors see US books and American odds.
  • The numbers move with the news, and say why. When a player is ruled out or a starting five changes, every teammate's prediction is re-run and the board shows the old number, the new one and the reason.
  • With and without. Any player's record beside or apart from any teammate, on the with/without page: the question a late scratch raises.
  • Every graded result stays on the record. The rules for what gets graded were written down before the season and each change is dated in how The Drop is chosen and graded. A withdrawn or void result is listed as withdrawn or void, never deleted.
  • Measured against the closing line. Each graded prop records the bookmaker's chance when we took it and its chance at tip-off. If the market moves our way more often than not, the numbers are finding something; a win–loss record alone can't show that.
  • Accuracy for everything, not only the winners. The track record reports the average miss and how often results land inside our ranges, across every player game.

Our record, including the bad part

  • Accuracy: across 5,797 player games this season, our points predictions missed by 4.3 on average, and 82% of results landed inside the 80% range.
  • The Drop (one player prop a day, 1 unit each): 24 covered, 11 not, +8.01 units. The market moved our way after posting on 90% of the 31 Drops with a closing line.
  • Graded props under the current rule (since 28 October): 136 covered, 72 not, +27.05 units, at an average price of 1.73, where 57.8% is break-even.
  • Graded props under the launch rule (20 to 27 October): 275 graded, about half covered, and they lost about 20 units. The model's stated chances were too high. We published the review, changed the rule, and left those results on the record.

A season is a small sample. Read the closing-line figures before the win–loss ones, and treat any record of a few hundred bets, ours included, as early evidence.

Where Drop Coverage is weaker

  • New. The public record starts on 20 October 2026. Sites that have run for years have more history to judge.
  • NBA only. No AFL, NRL, NFL or racing.
  • No game selections. We show each game's bookmaker line and each team's record against the spread, and we don't grade spreads or totals: our own back-test found no edge against the line there.
  • Not a line-shopping tool. We show the best Australian price for the side we favour. If comparing every bookmaker's price is the job, an odds comparison site does more.
  • One bet at a time. No same game multi builder yet.

Which should you use?

  • You want a number for a player and a reason to trust it: the board and the track record.
  • You want to see how often a player has cleared a line: our hit rates for the last 10 games against Australian lines, or a prop research tool for longer histories on US lines.
  • You want the best price on a bet you've already chosen: an odds comparison site.
  • You want a written preview of tonight's games: a tips site, or our NBA tips today page for the slate in Australian time.
