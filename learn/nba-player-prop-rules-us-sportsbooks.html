Explainer

What happens to an NBA player prop at US sportsbooks if the player doesn't play

At DraftKings, FanDuel, BetMGM, Caesars, Fanatics and theScore Bet, an NBA player prop is voided and your stake is returned if the player never gets on the court. Once he checks in, even for a second, the bet stands on his final stats, and overtime counts. A void leg in a same game parlay usually drops out and the parlay is repriced, rather than the whole ticket being voided. Early-injury refunds are a separate layer: they're promotions with their own windows and terms, not part of the house rules.

Read from each sportsbook's published house rules on 27 November 2026. House rules are filed state by state, so wording can differ where you live; we used the Ohio, New York and Massachusetts versions where noted. The rules shown on your bet slip and in your state's house rules always apply. Links to each source are at the bottom.

The short version

  • Ruled out before the game, or never checks in: void, stake returned, at all six books.
  • Plays, then gets hurt or ejected: the bet stands on what he did. Early-injury promotions (DraftKings Early Exit, Fanatics Fair Play, BetMGM BetDefense, FanDuel Bet Protect) can refund some of these bets, but each has its own window, bet types and payout form.
  • Comes off the bench: stands. None of the six books requires a player to start for a points, rebounds or assists prop to have action.
  • Overtime: counts for full-game props. Fourth-quarter and second-half player props are the usual exceptions.
  • Same game parlay with a void leg: the leg is removed and the parlay repriced at DraftKings, FanDuel, BetMGM and Fanatics. Caesars' Ohio rules contradict themselves on this, so check before you rely on it.
  • Stat corrections: DraftKings can resettle if the official stats change. FanDuel, Caesars and Fanatics stop recognising changes after a cut-off.

Early-injury refunds are promotions

The house rules at every book say a player who plays has action. Fanatics is the one that also gives itself room, in its house rules, to void props on a player who leaves injured in the first half. The refunds you hear about after a star limps off in the first quarter come from promotions layered on top: DraftKings Early Exit, Fanatics Fair Play, BetMGM BetDefense and FanDuel Bet Protect, plus FanDuel's paid Bet Protect+ add-on, which covers the whole game for a fee. They differ on the injury window (first quarter, first half, or the whole game, and often wider in the playoffs), whether live bets count, whether parlays are covered, whether you get cash or bonus bets, and which states they run in. Ejections, foul-outs and players who return after treatment are generally not covered. Check the promotion's own page on the day you bet, because these terms change during the season.

The Caesars contradiction

Caesars' Ohio house rules say two different things about a void leg in a single game parlay. One section says any voided or cancelled leg refunds the whole wager. Its same game parlay rules say the odds are recalculated on the remaining legs. Its New York rules say recalculated. If a same game parlay matters to you at Caesars, ask which rule they'll apply in your state.

Why "checks in" matters

A player listed as questionable who's a late scratch gets your stake back. One who starts, plays two minutes and leaves with a sprained ankle doesn't, unless a promotion covers him, and an over on his points will almost certainly lose. That's why late injury news matters so much for props, and why our predictions are rerun when a player is ruled out or a starting five changes, and again once the starters are confirmed.

Stat corrections

The NBA corrects box scores for a day or two after a game. A rebound or assist taken off or added later can decide a bet that sat right on the line. DraftKings reserves the right to resettle when the official stats differ from the ones it used. FanDuel ignores information that arrives more than 48 hours after settlement, Caesars stops recognising score changes after a cut-off the day after the game, and Fanatics treats results as final 24 hours after they're announced. We re-read every box score for three days after each game for the same reason.

How we grade our own

For The Drop and the graded props, a player who doesn't take the court makes the result void, the way the sportsbooks treat it, and a void is never counted as covered or not. The track record counts voids apart from the wins and losses.
